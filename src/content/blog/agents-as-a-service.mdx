### Context

If your idea of an agent is a .md file, you’re doing it wrong. 
If agent means giving it tools, you’re still doing it wrong.

Agents should be frameworks.

**TL;DR:** Push as much of the work as you can into scripts, make the AI prove everything else, and run the whole thing as a service. Do that and Gemma4:e4b gets within spitting distance of Opus.

### Background

I work for a SOAR platform.
Automation is my bread and butter. It's what makes me tick. Solving problems as a wannabe developer, with way less gatekeeping.

Early days of AI popularity meant typing into a chat.
Early AI adoption across the industry meant pass-the-prompt.

Then there was prompt engineering. Most of the early website chatbots of 2020-2024~ were made this way. Your prompt is just prepended with instructions before being sent to AI. Still pass-the-prompt. Still slop.

Then Claude Code came along and introduced Skills, Agents, Tools, Hooks, etc.

These are on-demand sets of static text files that hold information like the prompt above (and sometimes scripts!). They get pulled in before, during, and after your prompt. We're still performing Prompt Engineering; it's just automated and obscured away, used only when necessary.

These days, the secret sauce lies in how you curate this information before, during, and after your AI prompts. That's building a "harness." Likewise, "scaffolding" is taking all of your work, your notes, your -isms, and having AI plagiarize you.

Create a harness with good scaffolding, and your AI will act as a "second brain."

### Workforce Amplification

Using AI as a second brain is an abstract form of Automation. Instead of sitting down and writing a script for a niche, specific-purpose task... give your harnessed, scaffolded AI a simple prompt and it will create said script in a fraction of the time, in damn near the same way you would (since it's plagiarizing you).

When computers became mainstream, they took mundane tasks and churned through them in a fraction of the time by means of... software. That opened the door to more consumer interactions and transactions. Suddenly Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability became extremely important for hardware and software. Now you need dedicated roles and departments for up-time (fixing, planning), innovation (updates, features), disaster recovery (what if everything blows up???), etc. All to keep productivity up: more output, more money.

When businesses started getting bogged down by these departments, and the <RedTape>red tape</RedTape> meant to keep productivity from ever dropping... enter "Shadow IT." Despite the name, it really just means people finding ways to make their jobs easier, outside of the <RedTape>red tape</RedTape>. It's easier, cheaper, and quicker to write a script that glues together two pieces of software than it is to sit there copy/pasting from one into the other.

Cybersecurity departments are fraught with this type of tech debt. To name a few of the tools: SIEM, EDR, Firewall (+ Email Firewall), Proxy, VPN, etc.
That's where a SOAR platform comes into play. It glues these technologies together to make responding to incidents quicker and easier. Workforce Amplification.

What kick-started my career was one of the worst alerts we got at a Financial Institution. The SIEM would alert on a possible fraud manager and their money mules cashing fake checks. The SIEM was riddled with tech debt, and no one would give me access (or time) to fix it. Whenever we received a Fraud alert, we'd go into the SIEM, pull down the logs and all activity for the account(s), de-duplicate parts of the logs (IP Address, HTTP User-Agent, Accept-Language, etc.), and compile a hand-written list of "Red Flags." Then we'd compile the report and email it over to the Fraud department. We only had a 2hr~ window to stop the fraudsters before they withdrew the money and ran.

"Speedrunning" these Fraud alerts took about 18~ minutes. That was too long, especially when we'd get a dozen Fraud alerts per day, not counting the Phishing and Malware alerts.

So I made a script that took the .csv report from the SIEM, glued together a few tools like <IpLookupLink>Scamalytics</IpLookupLink> and [MaxMind](https://www.maxmind.com/en/geoip-web-services-demo), compiled a deterministic list of "Red Flags," and created the email. Now the Fraud alerts took 2mins~.

Workforce Amplification. It only took a disgruntled Analyst such as myself a few dozen extra hours, between escalations.

Nowadays, I just feed my heavily customized AI a prompt, "Make me Y," and off it goes... No more staying late and working overtime to create a script that glues together a few systems. AI will just do that. Workforce Amplification. Now what's gluing services together is AI. Who needs a SOAR platform when AI can glue things together? (Hint: <RedTape>red tape</RedTape>.)

### Agents as a Service

As a business user... I'm getting bored of AI.
Modern uses rely on harnesses, scaffolding, and Loop Engineering.
Loop Engineering is the newest buzzword: you build the thing that prompts the agent *for* you. Stop conditions it can actually check, verifier sub-agents, token budgets, and a point where it kicks back to a human ([Lulla et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21884)).
I've already got AI doing the majority of my job. I just manage chats all day, and AI creates the glue that binds together business processes and services.

For Claude, Cursor, Kiro... "Agents" give AI a personality. At best. It's fun to create a bunch of agents and watch them rip apart a problem or argue with each other. It's not so fun watching how many tokens that burns, or realizing the outcome isn't that different from a well-structured harness.

Other "agents" or "agentic" systems are just reusing prompt-engineering tricks, with limited access to tools and zero account-level learning.

When I use AI, I'm not doing anything novel. Everything I have it work on is stuff that could just be turned into scripts. Most of the time it creates scripts to work from anyway. Why not centralize this?

We need to go back to classic Machine Learning approaches: have an overarching AI that can call out to specific-purpose AI models. Better yet, we make a framework for said specific-purpose AI models.

### Stress Testing

That said, these are heavily dependent on the AI model that's running them. I created stress tests to gauge how well the models perform at planning and content creation. Before I created the agents, this was usually where things fell flat.

Two different tests in here, don't mix them up:
- **Validation (Framework #1):** the scripts do the heavy lifting and the model just fills in the gaps. That's where Gemma4:e4b got ~80% of Opus 4.8.
- **Planning & Creation (the table below):** the model has to plan and build from scratch with the Rules, Skills, and Tools. That's where the gap between models really shows.

The task: take a simple prompt and build content in a SOAR platform.

It started as "Create a playbook for URL OSINT." Purposefully vague and open-ended. Later it got expanded to "Create a Solution that will perform URL OSINT with [URLScan.io](https://urlscan.io), Lookalike Domains ([duo labs](https://github.com/duo-labs/lookalike-domains)), URLUnfurl ([dfirblog](https://dfir.blog/unfurl/))."

After running Cursor, I had to add stages to the stress test to make sure the environment gets cleaned up between runs and the model can't access prior chats. Composer 2.5 "fast" ran for 13hrs after that, trying to find content to steal and looping endlessly.

Prior to these frameworks, it took Opus 4.8 about 2hrs~ to complete. Now it's only a few minutes.

### Key Takeaways

1. **Scripts first.** If it can be a script, make it a script. The AI only gets what's left over.
2. **Silo the AI.** If the Prompt can see what the scripts already found, it just agrees with them and you never find out what they missed.
3. **Split the request.** Input and User-Ask get separated by a script. Most of the loop never sees the ask, so it can't steer how the evidence gets read.
4. **Everything goes through normalization.** Nothing hits the learning queue raw.
5. **Centralize it.** Same reasons as Shadow IT: standardize, audit, and share the learnings with the whole department.
6. **The framework did more than the model.** **~1.8m tokens** per run before the framework, regardless of model. **~47k** after, inside a dedicated environment & pipeline. That's how Gemma4:e4b gets within spitting distance of Opus 4.8.
7. **Opus is still the best.** GPT-5.6 is fine. Grok is behind. Cursor is a party trick.
